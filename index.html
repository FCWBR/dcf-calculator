06

Definitions & ratio reference

Key terms used throughout this model — from core DCF mechanics to valuation ratios. Filter by category or browse all. Adyen's current figures (FY 2024) are referenced where relevant.

Free Cash FlowFCF
The cash a company generates after paying for capital expenditures needed to maintain or expand its asset base. It represents the actual cash available to shareholders, debt holders, and for reinvestment — the purest measure of a company's earning power.
FCF = Operating Cash Flow − Capital Expenditure
Adyen FY 2024: €1,603M total / €51.45/share
Model input
Discount Rate / WACCWACC
The Weighted Average Cost of Capital — the minimum return a company must earn to satisfy all capital providers (equity holders and debt holders). Used to discount future cash flows to present value. A higher WACC produces a lower intrinsic value estimate.
WACC = (E/V × Re) + (D/V × Rd × (1−T))
Typical range for growth equities: 8–12%
Model input
Terminal Growth RateTGR
The assumed perpetual growth rate of free cash flow beyond the forecast period (year 10 onwards). Should never exceed long-run nominal GDP growth, as no company can grow faster than the economy indefinitely. Adyen's eurozone context supports a 2.5–3.5% range.
Terminal Value = FCF₁₀ × (1 + g) / (WACC − g)
Rule of thumb: ≤ 3% for developed-market companies
Model input
Terminal ValueTV
The present value of all cash flows beyond the 10-year explicit forecast period, calculated using the Gordon Growth Model. In high-growth companies like Adyen, terminal value often accounts for 50–70% of total intrinsic value — making the terminal growth rate assumption critically important.
TV = (FCF₁₀ × (1+g)) / (WACC − g)
Then discounted: TV PV = TV / (1+WACC)¹⁰
Model input
Intrinsic ValueIV
The estimated true or fundamental value of a security, independent of its current market price. Derived from discounting all future cash flows to present value and adding net assets. If market price is below intrinsic value, the stock may be undervalued — the foundation of value investing.
IV = PV of Phase 1 FCFs + PV of Phase 2 FCFs
       + PV of Terminal Value + Net Cash/Share
Key concept
Present ValuePV
The current worth of a future sum of money, discounted at the WACC to account for the time value of money. A euro received today is worth more than a euro received in 10 years — PV quantifies exactly how much more, adjusting for risk and opportunity cost.
PV = Future Cash Flow / (1 + WACC)ⁿ
Where n = number of years into the future
Key concept
Two-Phase DCF ModelDCF
A discounted cash flow model that separates projections into two explicit growth phases before applying a terminal value. Phase 1 (years 1–5) uses a higher near-term growth estimate; Phase 2 (years 6–10) assumes a lower, more sustainable rate as the company matures.
Phase 1 (yr 1–5): 18% growth assumption
Phase 2 (yr 6–10): 12% growth assumption
Key concept
Net Cash PositionNCA
Cash and equivalents minus total financial debt. A positive net cash position is added to the DCF-derived equity value because this cash belongs to shareholders above and beyond the company's operating value. Adyen holds ~€10B in cash with minimal debt — a significant add-back of ~€321/share.
Net Cash/Share = (Cash − Debt) / Shares
Adyen FY 2024: ~€10B cash ÷ 31.16M shares = €321
Key concept
Margin of SafetyMoS
Coined by Benjamin Graham, the margin of safety is the discount to intrinsic value at which you are willing to buy. It provides a buffer against errors in your assumptions, unforeseen events, and model uncertainty. A 20–30% MoS is typical; higher MoS is preferred for volatile or uncertain businesses.
Buy price = Intrinsic Value × (1 − MoS%)
At 20% MoS: IV of €1,200 → buy at €960 or below
Risk & safety
Sensitivity AnalysisSA
A technique that examines how intrinsic value changes as key assumptions vary. Because DCF outputs are highly sensitive to WACC and growth rate inputs, a sensitivity table shows a range of plausible outcomes rather than a single point estimate — critical for understanding model uncertainty.
Small changes compound: +1% WACC on a 10-yr model
can reduce intrinsic value by 10–20%
Risk & safety
Price-to-Earnings RatioP/E
The most widely used valuation multiple. Shows how many euros investors are paying per euro of earnings. A high P/E may reflect high growth expectations or overvaluation; a low P/E may signal value or declining prospects. Compare against sector peers and historical averages.
P/E = Stock Price / Earnings Per Share (EPS)
Adyen FY 2024: ~€886 / ~€28 EPS ≈ ~32×
Valuation ratio
Price-to-Free-Cash-FlowP/FCF
Often preferred over P/E for capital-light businesses because FCF is harder to manipulate than net income. Shows how many euros the market pays per euro of free cash flow generated. Particularly relevant for Adyen, whose FCF conversion is exceptionally high (~50% of net revenue).
P/FCF = Market Cap / Free Cash Flow
Adyen FY 2024: €27.6B mkt cap / €1.6B FCF ≈ ~17×
Valuation ratio
EV / EBITDAEV/EBITDA
Enterprise value divided by earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation. Capital-structure neutral — useful for comparing companies with different debt levels. Common in M&A and cross-sector comparisons. For fintech payments, typical ranges are 20–50× for high-growth platforms.
EV/EBITDA = (Mkt Cap + Debt − Cash) / EBITDA
Adyen FY 2024 (approx): ~22–26×
Valuation ratio
Price-to-Sales RatioP/S
Market capitalisation divided by annual revenue (or net revenue for payment companies). Useful for early-stage or high-growth companies without consistent earnings. For payments platforms like Adyen, net revenue (take-rate revenue after interchange) is the relevant top line, not gross processed volume.
P/S = Market Cap / Annual Net Revenue
Adyen FY 2024: €27.6B / €2.3B net rev ≈ ~12×
Valuation ratio
FCF YieldFCF%
Free cash flow per share divided by share price — the inverse of P/FCF. Expressed as a percentage, it shows the cash return you receive relative to the price paid. A higher FCF yield generally indicates better value. Comparable to a bond's yield for equity investors.
FCF Yield = (FCF Per Share / Price) × 100
Adyen FY 2024: €51.45 / €886 ≈ 5.8%
Valuation ratio
Return on Invested CapitalROIC
Measures how efficiently a company generates profits from its invested capital. A ROIC consistently above WACC means the company is creating shareholder value — one of the most important long-term quality indicators. Adyen's capital-light model yields exceptionally high ROIC.
ROIC = NOPAT / Invested Capital
Adyen FY 2024 (approx): >40% — well above WACC
